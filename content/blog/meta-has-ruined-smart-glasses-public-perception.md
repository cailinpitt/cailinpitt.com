I just finished reading this [excellent article](https://www.theverge.com/report/982414/meta-glasses-work-surveillance-labor-security) about people using Meta's smart glasses to harass workers at their jobs for social media content, and it brought up a lot of frustration I've been feeling about smart glasses recently. 

As an emerging product line, smart glasses are extremely fascinating to me as a concept. I think they have (or had?) the potential to be more useful for everyday tasks than smart watches. I've used various Apple Watches over the years and I've always been sort of _meh_ about them. I think they're helpful for viewing info on the go and being able to accomplish small tasks without needing to pull my phone out, but I've also found them easy to forget about and sometimes a little too notification heavy. Smart watches were exciting to me because they actually feel useful and more natural on the go instead of a watch. Sometimes I don't want to strap a computer to my wrist! I liked the possibility of using smart glasses to snap a picture of something cool instead of having to pull my phone out of my pocket, especially while biking. Smart glasses with screens felt really exciting too – I could see myself using a navigation app in my glasses to know where I needed to go instead of having to look down at my phone.

Unfortunately Meta is a horrible and evil company, with zero vision or thought when it comes to products and how they'll be used. Because they were basically the first to mass market with smart glasses they set the tone of how they'd be publicly perceived, and they let their products get used by losers with bad intentions. Meta has also been extremely resistant to doing any sort of moderation of videos being posted on Instagram where people are using their smart glasses to harass people. Meta also hasn't done anything about the fact that users have figured out ways to hide the recording indicator lights on their smart glasses so people can be filmed without their knowledge (I've personally seen ads on Instagram for stickers you can purchase to hide the lights, and have reported them to no avail). Between this and [Flock cameras](https://www.aclu.org/campaigns-initiatives/get-the-flock-out) I think people are (rightfully) sick of personal surveillance, and smart glasses may be dead. Apple seems poised to release [AirPods with cameras](https://www.theverge.com/tech/981326/apple-airpods-with-cameras-demo-video-leak) next month that are simply intended to identify surroundings and don't seem to have the ability for users to take photos, and people are upset even about this type of product. 

There have been rumours of Apple releasing smart glasses at some point, and Google is [planning to release smart glasses](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-xr-io-2026/) later this year. I had hoped to buy Apple's version at some point, but I think I'd likely receive backlash if someone saw me wearing them in public even if I had good intentions. That's assuming smart glasses even continue to get released at this point in today's cultural environment. I can't even be upset at people who are opposed to smart glasses, but I am sad about something being hampered that I think could have been extremely helpful to me and others.
