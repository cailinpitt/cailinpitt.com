Today I was reading a [Business Insider article](https://www.businessinsider.com/amazon-boomerang-hiring-recruiting-former-employees-laid-off-2026-9) (archive.ph link [here](https://archive.ph/DH37v)) about Amazon actively engaging with the pool of 30,000 people it laid off over the past year to rehire them as the company tries to compete in the AI/ML space. I found this to be an extremely pathetic situation that affirmed how I've felt about the leadership of big tech companies for a while: utterly clueless people.

Boomerangs in tech (an employee being rehired by a company after quitting or being laid off) are not a new phenomenon – I knew several people at my previous employer who quit for various reasons, only for me to see them show up in various Slack channels again later. I also know of people who have been laid off at a place, only to be rehired because the company realized "oh shit we actually needed this person." The latter situation seems to be happening quite a bit in tech these days, and I think it's a perfect example of how no one leading big tech companies seems to be making decisions with a long term lens. I think it also shows a lot of leadership truly has no idea what they're really doing, and they're just throwing things at the wall to see what sticks.

Barring a legitimate reason (like financial ruin/looming bankruptcy), laying thousands of people off at once is a sign of weakness and unintelligence. First of all, there is zero chance that all of those people's jobs were able to be evaluated as truly unnecessary – companies that do this are simply engaging in guesswork and crossing their fingers that they're getting rid of the "right" people. The scale of people involved is simply too large! Mass layoffs hurt surviving employees who then have to pick up slack and readjust to new organizational structures and operating realities. I also find mass layoffs to be morally abhorrent: people work to put a roof over their heads and pay their bills: if you're suddenly cutting someone's livelihood off you need to be damn sure you're making a good decision. Lastly, companies who boomerang laid off employees are shooting themselves in the foot: you've wasted valuable time firing and then rehiring someone, and there's a good chance they'll decline to boomerang because the experience of being laid off left a bad taste in their mouth!

The thing I find most pathetic about mass layoffs specifically in the era of AI is how proud tech companies are in the moment when they do mass layoffs, compared to how sheepish and quiet they are when working to hire people back. I talked a little about this [here](https://cailinpitt.com/blog/2026/8/01/everyone-is-so-annoying-about-ai), where a lot of companies did layoffs because they thought AI would be able to replace people en masse, only to quickly learn this wasn't the case. Intelligent leaders don't make huge rash decisions with large implications at breakneck speed. They wait for research and facts to back up their beliefs before executing.
