The Democrats had historic wins last night across the country. There are too many notable results for me to even mention (ex. the last time Kansas sent a Dem to the Senate was 96 years ago), but two states close to my heart had really important results: both Ohio and Georgia flipped blue virtually overnight, with dems now running statewide offices.

Keisha Lance Bottoms winning the Georgia Gubernatorial race feels significant to me for a lot of reasons. During my time in Georgia, it was always very clear that republican state leaders did not care for Atlanta and viewed it negatively. This was evident in a lot of (non-exhaustive, off the top of my head) ways: how the state forced Atlanta to "open back up" early during Covid, the Buckhead City Movement coming dangerously close to fruition (a movement fueled by many in the statehouse, which would have destroyed Atlanta fiscally and in so many other ways), the state providing virtually no financial assistance to MARTA, and the many ways state leaders have worked to bully and strip Atlanta leaders of independence and power over the years. Atlanta metro is the economic engine of Georgia and it makes zero sense for state leaders to try and handicap Atlanta at every turn – yet they do so for various reasons, racism being one of them. It also feels really special to have a Governor of a state shaped by so much racism, [whose ancestors were literally enslaved here](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/11/keisha-lance-bottoms-crawfordville-governors-mansion/) ~150 years ago.

There isn't a better representation of this dynamic than the gates erected outside the statehouse, spurred by protests in 2020 related to police brutality and the killing of Rayshard Brooks roughly 1.8 miles from the statehouse steps. Today, the statehouse feels like a fortress that is disconnected from Atlanta. It feels ominous to be near it, even while people inside are making decisions that impact Atlanta.

![](/images/libbing-out/statehouse-gates.webp)

*Photo from [TYMETAL](https://www.tymetal.com/project/public-service-buildings/georgia-capitol-project/), the contractor who built the statehouse gates*

Georgia having a Governor from Atlanta feels like the first step in culturally changing Georgia to be a place where Atlanta is embraced by the state and not reviled. I'm hoping that the new crop of state leaders are able to improve the quality of life for Georgians, and end many of the racist policies and practices that have ruled Georgia over the last several decades of republican control.
