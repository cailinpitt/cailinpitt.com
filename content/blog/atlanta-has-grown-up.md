I lived in Atlanta for several years before moving to Chicago in 2024. I've been spending some time there this summer and I've been blown away by the amount of progress the city has made in regards to bike and pedestrian infrastructure in two years, particularly with the Beltline. If you're unfamiliar with it, the [Beltline](https://beltline.org/about-us/history/) is a project to take unused rail lines and create a 22 mile trail around Atlanta's urban core. Solely thinking of the Beltline as a trail is a disservice though because it's a lot more than that – a ton of new dense housing developments have been built on/around it, as well as restaurants and parks and offices. It's linking parts of the city together that were always difficult to get between, especially when the interstate system was built in the 1960s.

The Beltline has been under development for years and the entire loop won't be finished until ~2030, but during my time in Atlanta there were a few completed parts (namely the southwest and eastern sections). A few parts were in an interim state (like the south section), which just meant they were "open" but it was just a dirt trail you couldn't really bike on unless you had a gravel bike and weren't afraid of loose dirt. A ton of the Beltline has been completed during my two years away (I believe the Beltline is officially ~85% complete at this point) and the only sections left to be completed are the northern sections around Lindbergh and southern Buckhead. Last week I biked the entire Beltline loop (all of the open sections + the unopened ones) and was truly blown away by how much progress has been made and how transformative the Beltline is and will continue to be to Atlanta. You can now bike from SweetWater Brewing all the way to Blandtown without needing to get on a single street and share space with cars.

When I lived in Atlanta I sort of disliked the Beltline because I thought people there were annoying about it. There's an archetype of White Affluent Atlanta Resident who is obsessed with the Beltline (in particular the east side section) and basically spends all their time on it and doesn't really want to do or see anything else in the city. That has never been my vibe, as I always thought there was more to Atlanta than just the Beltline. However, biking the entire Beltline after being away and seeing how many people were on it made me go _holy shit, this is a big deal and I think I get it now_. Atlanta has never been viewed as a progressive city in the urbanism sense – it's known for having horrible traffic congestion and sprawl. It feels nuts something this transformative is being built here and not in a different city like Chicago or NYC. 

Besides the Beltline, it felt like everything that [really pissed me off as a bicyclist and pedestrian during my time in Atlanta](https://howidfixatlanta.com/2023/03/build-more-bike-infrastructure/) has been fixed. The city made the West Peachtree bike lane (previously ridiculously thin and scary to ride on) wide, extended it a bit, and added a lot of paint to it. After ~15 years of talking about it, the city finally built and opened a bike lane on Juniper. 14th Street now has a bike lane. Lots of existing bike lanes (like 10th and Edgewood) have bollards now. The Jackson Street bike lane has been upgraded to make it contraflow and prevent drivers from parking their cars on it 24/7. These are just random things I noticed with my eyes, I know a lot of other work has been done that I haven't gotten to see yet.

Seeing all of this progress made me really proud of Atlanta. It's also punching above its weight – the city has built way more bike infrastructure than Chicago has over the last few years. From what I've heard from my Atlanta friends, there are a lot of projects still in the pipeline and the progress won't be slowing down.
