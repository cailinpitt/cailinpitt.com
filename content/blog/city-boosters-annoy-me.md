I've lived in two cities with big booster culture, Atlanta and Chicago. By booster culture, I mean residents who are _obsessed_ with the city, think it is underrated in the eyes of the world, and are practically devoted to spreading the gospel of it. 

People in Atlanta love Atlanta. I actually do think Atlanta is underrated and has a ton to offer, but I think it flies under the radar simply because it is in the South (a part of the country Americans generally don't consider to be interesting). Similarly, Chicagoans love Chicago and sort of have a chip on their shoulders due to feeling like it's unfairly maligned and ignored due to NYC and LA taking up the spotlight.

I think it's truly wonderful to love where you live. However, something that annoyed me during the end of my time in Atlanta and even now as my time in Chicago wraps up is that I think boosterism can be harmful if not balanced correctly. Chicago is an incredible city I love, and I will always cherish the years I had here. It's also a deeply broken city with a lot of issues. To start: housing costs are skyrocketing, it's one of the most segregated cities in the country, and the city is spending an [obscene amount of money every year dealing with police misconduct](https://news.wttw.com/2026/06/17/chicago-has-spent-least-225m-resolve-police-misconduct-lawsuits-just-6-months-year). **I find that most of the people who are obsessed with opining about how incredible this city is, tend to ignore the real problems here and only want to focus on the positives.** Cities need boosters who love where they live, while simultaneously advocating and working to make it better. Loving your home but choosing to ignore its problems isn't true love: I think it's just infatuation.
